Genetic algorithm ABSTRACT  
As carbon-free and renewable fuels, ammonia and hydrogen are promising fuels to be utilized in combustion 
devices. For this, the genetic algorithm was employed to reduce and optimize the mechanism for ammonia/ 
hydrogen mixture, which was aimed to develop a compact and reliable skeletal mechanism for real engine 
simulation with ammonia as fuel. During the progress of GA reduction, the extracted sub-mechanisms was 
constrained high fidelity with original mechanism on predicting the combustion characteristics through the 
definition of penalty functions. Besides, the number of reactions and computer calculation time of the sub- 
mechanism were simultaneously tracked to reduce the chemical stiffness. The resulting mechanism contained 
29 species and 63 reactions and was streamlined to be coupled into complex CFD calculation. The reduced 
mechanism was adjusted to better capture fundamental combustion data and the mechanism optimization was 
finished automatically through GA operation. To verify the reliability of the optimized mechanism, the experi -
mental data including ignition delay times, laminar flame speed, JSR species concentration, premixed flame 
structure and HCCI engine in-cylinder pressure at various conditions was utilized to evaluate the performance of 
the models. As a result, the optimized mechanism was capable to reproduce those measurements and was 
credible for engine combustion simulation.   
1.Introduction 
The combustion of fossil fuels produces greenhouse gases, mainly 
CO2, which will accelerate global warming and jeopardize human living 
space [1]. As carbon-free, renewable and sustainable fuels, the ammonia 
and hydrogen are considered to be promising to substitute fossil fuels [2, 
3]. Ammonia has some problems such as low burning rate and unstable 
combustion, which will be improved with the mixing of hydrogen [4,5]. 
What ’s more, ammonia is regarded as one of the ideal carriers for 
hydrogen due to its low reactivity. It was reported that 1 m3 of liquid 
ammonia had the capacity to store 108 kg of H2 at a temperature of 
20 •C and pressure of 8.6 atm [6]. Due to the high hydrogen content in 
ammonia, more heat could be released during ammonia oxidation. It can 
be used as a chemical storage medium for hydrogen and also release 
hydrogen by decomposition [7]. Exploring the feasibility of comple -
mentary application of ammonia-hydrogen mixtures in internal com-
bustion engines (ICE), gas turbines and other practical power engines 
attracted a lot of attention [8,9]. 
To reduce emissions in the transportation industry currently, the 
compression ignition (CI) engines and spark ignition (SI) engines fueled with ammonia and hydrogen mixture was studied to provide stable and 
reliable power output. Dimitriou et al. [10] reviewed the research on 
dual-fuel compression ignition engine operation with ammonia and 
other highly reactive fuels like diesel. Pochet et al. [11] successfully 
conducted experiments on the ammonia-hydrogen mixture HCCI en-
gines with an effective compression ratio of 22:1 and pointed out that 
the ammonia-hydrogen blending ratio greatly affected the engine output 
except for the indicated efficiency. Although it was difficult to ignite 
ammonia alone, the SI engine worked satisfactorily with low and 
moderate hydrogen addition [12]. Frigo et al. [13] confirmed the ne-
cessity of blending hydrogen for ignition improvement and an increase 
in combustion velocity when ammonia was applied in gasoline engines, 
in addition, the appropriate proportion depended on the situation. 
The foundation chemistry mechanism of ammonia was laid by Miller 
et al. [14] and Lindstedt et al. Unlike methane and hydrogen, the clarity 
of understanding of the chemical kinetic behavior of ammonia remains a 
great challenge, especially at low temperatures (T D1200 K) and under 
dilute mixtures. A number of chemical kinetic mechanisms for ammonia, 
ammonia/hydrocarbon mixture were proposed and verified, e.g. Glar-
borg et al. [15], Klippenstein et al. [16], Mathieu et al. [17], Me’vel et al. 
[18], Song et al. [19], Stagni et al. [20], Zhang et al. [21], Otomo et al.

The mechanism needs to be reduced while providing a reasonable 
accuracy to pursue a proper and cost-effective numerical simulation, 
especially direct numerical simulation (DNS) and large eddy simulation 
(LES) of a real engine. In addition, numerical simulation for combustion 
behavior in the dual-fuel engine may face a heavy burden when a 
detailed ammonia mechanism is combined with the mechanism of 
another active fuel like dimethyl ether. The reduced or skeletal mech -
anism of ammonia/hydrogen mixture was relatively limited in literature 
works. Xiao et al. [26] reduced the scale of the ammonia/hydrogen 
mechanism for 2D gas turbine LES. The mechanism reduction was per-
formed in Chemkin II software using the directed relation graph (DRG) 
method. The reduced mechanism developed by Duynslaegher et al. [27] 
for low-pressure conditions was verified only under species concentra -
tion. Rocha et al. [28] considered that the current ammonia/hydrogen 
combustion mechanism had the potential for further simplification with 
high fidelity. Three reduced mechanisms with 50–70 element reactions 
were proposed based on the mechanism of Mathieu [17]’s, Okafor [29]’s 
and Otomo [22]’s. Li et al. [30] concentrated on extracting and reducing 
the NH3 and NH3/H2 mechanism from the large-scale mechanism with 
directed relation graph with error propagation (DRGEP) and DRGEP 
with sensitivity analysis (DRGEPSA) strategy. Still, 28 species and 213 reactions were included in his NH3/H2 combustion mechanism. Cai 
et al. [31] developed reduced NH3/H2 mechanisms for ignition delay 
and laminar flame speed prediction with Chemkin-Pro 2019 using a 
method of direct relation graph with error propagation. The calculated 
IDTs by his mechanism was compared with experimental data only 
under high temperature and pure ammonia fueled. From the above 
papers, compact kinetic mechanism of ammonia/hydrogen required 
more extensive validation against not only original mechanism but also 
experimental data. Especially, the evaluation of the ignition delay times 
(IDTs) at both low- and high-temperature should be considered, which is 
partially regarded as ICE simulations of practical interest [32]. In 
addition, the skeletal mechanism with the small number of reaction rate 
parameters can be optimized to achieve a better mechanism 
configuration. 
Attractive approaches to the reduced mechanisms such as the 
decoupling method, DRG, quasi-steady-state assumption (QSSA), 
element flux analysis (EF), and sensitivity analysis all require the user to 
understand the detailed chemistry knowledge. The method of uncer -
tainty quantification was used for mechanism optimization in order to 
minimize the deviation between calculation and measurement in Wang 
[33] ’s study. The mechanism optimization by manual iteration strongly 
depends on path analysis and experience [34]. Therefore, researchers 
introduce genetic algorithms (GA) to develop a simple and robust 
reduction methodology. The optimal solution search process is directed 
by the overall objective function, and unimportant reactions would be 
automatically removed in the iterative algorithm. The penalty function 
is used to constrain CPU calculation time, avoiding stiff and slow 
converging mechanism set. The method was proved to be effective in 
previous studies [35–37]. GA can also be exploited in optimizing the 
reaction rate constants to decrease the gap between model predictions 
and experimental measurements [32,34,38]. In addition, a traditional 
algorithm for finding an optimal solution like gradient-based algorithms 
and the solution mapping method was unsuitable for mechanism tun-
ning due to the complexity and non-linearity of the chemical mechanism 
[39]. Chang et al. [34] optimized the skeletal oxidation mechanism of 
n-pentanol by tuning the Arrhenius pre-exponential factors of C4-Cn 
sub-mechanism using a multi-objective genetic algorithm. As a result, 
predictions by the optimized mechanism model were well agreed with 
the experimental IDTs, laminar flame speed and species concentrations 
in JSRs. 
In this study, the ammonia/hydrogen reduced mechanism was 
developed by genetic algorithm-based method. Penalty functions were 
designed to guide the removal of uninterested reactions, in which not 
only the fidelity between the streamlined mechanism and the complete 
mechanism was considered but also the CPU computational time was 
limited to reduce the stiffness of the mechanism set. The reduced 
mechanism for ammonia/hydrogen mixture was also optimized based 
on GA for better prediction of the ignition delay times and laminar flame 
speed. Finally, the mechanisms were validated by contrast with exper -
imental data from fundamental combustion reactors and in-cylinder 
pressure profiles from the HCCI engine. The main content of this 
article was presented in Fig. 1. 
The contribution of this paper to the related field is mainly twofold. 
First, the genetic algorithm, a simple yet robust method, was tested for 
reducing and optimizing ammonia/hydrogen mechanism, in which the 
role of some elementary reactions still was unknown currently. Second, 
not only developing a compact ammonia/hydrogen simplified mecha -
nism for engine CFD simulation, but also performing mechanism opti-
mization to achieve a better mechanism configuration. The extensive 
validation proved confidence in the reliability of the reduced mecha -

32.Methodology 
2.1. Genetic algorithm 
Genetic algorithm is an adaptive heuristic mathematical technique 
based on biological evolution principles. A set of initial populations are 
created firstly in the solution space of an optimal problem in GA. Then 
the behavior of each individual is evaluated by the fitness function, 
which correlates with the optimization problem and therefore guides 
genetic operators so that individuals in the next generation of pop-
ulations behave better. The iterative process between generations is also 
the search process of the optimal solution, and the process terminates 
when an established criterion is met or a given number of generations 
are gained [35]. The advantage of GA includes fast and stochastic search 
capability, potential parallelism, and search using evaluation function 
heuristics. 
The genetic algorithm procedure adopted is shown in Fig. 2 and the 
search process for the optimal solution in this study consists of the 
following steps:  
1. Initialization, where a set of initial individuals (single chromosome 
in one individual) with reaction information are generated.  
2. Evaluation, where the performance of individual is evaluated and 
corresponding fitness is assigned to each individual.  
3. Selection, where the chromosome of the fittest individual is retained 
for next-generation (Elitist). The other individuals compete for the 
opportunity to retain chromosomes for the next generation.  
4. Crossover, where the chosen chromosomes exchange information to 
create a new mechanism set for the next generation.

2.2. Implementation details of mechanism reduction 
The kinetic mechanism reduction method based on genetic algo-
rithms developed by Sikalo et al. [37] is adopted in this study, and the 
same operation has appeared in the literature [35,36]. Therefore, a brief 
description of the implementation method is presented here. The GA is 
used to eliminate reactions instead of species. It is possible to achieve a 
more streamlined structure by removing unimportant reaction paths 
compared with deleting the species. Therefore, each gene corresponds to 
one reaction and the gene takes the value 0 (meaning reaction absent) or 
1 (meaning reaction presence). The process of chromosome encoding 
and decoding can also be ignored in the algorithm by this way. Each 
chromosome has the length corresponding to the number of reactions in 
the full mechanism. Each individual represents a kind of 
sub-mechanism. For initial population, all the chromosomes are set to 
the same and all genes assigned as the value of 1 represent the original 
mechanism. This approach is designed to avoid the loss of key reactions 
in the first generation. The iteration between generations seeks the 
optimal reaction subset under user-defined evaluation rules. 
The evaluation function (objective or fitness function) is an essential 
part of this optimization technique. Different sub-mechanisms are 
selected as the resulting reduced mechanism under various evaluation 
function definitions. Three parts including the accuracy, size, and stiff-
ness of the reduced mechanism are taken into consideration. The ac-
curacy of sub-mechanism is evaluated by simulating combustion in a 
homogeneous reactor, where ignition delay time and steady-state tem-
perature are selected as the properties of interest. Generally speaking, 
the fewer reactions in the mechanism are, the smaller the calculation 
cost is, yet not including slow converging mechanisms. That is why the 
CPU calculation time is also tracked to assess the stiffness of the sub- 
mechanism. According to Sikalo et al. [37], the ignition delay time 
error and the steady-state temperature error are defined as Equations (1) 
and (2) respectively due to the logarithmic expression steep conver -
gence toward the optimal point. The mechanism size and cost evaluation 
function are defined as Equations (3) and (4). The global objective 
function is defined in Equation (5).

2.3. Implementation details of mechanism optimization 
In the application of mechanism reduction above, the sub- 
mechanism and the original mechanism are required to be as consis -
tent as possible while predicting the heat release and ignition delay 
characteristics of ammonia/hydrogen. Apart from ignition delay char-
acteristics, the flame propagation velocity of hydrogen mixture was also 
concerned here. In order to decrease the deviation between the calcu -
lations by the resulting reduced mechanism and measurements in ex-
periments, the reduced mechanism was improved by tuning Arrhenius 
pre-exponential factor A based on the genetic algorithm. 
The goal of the optimization is the A-factor of all reactions in reduced 
mechanism except for the hydrogen sub-mechanism and the pressure- 
dependent reactions, including 45 reactions. It is considered that 
hydrogen oxidation has been studied sufficiently and the current 
hydrogen mechanism is quite mature [20]. The pressure-dependent re-
actions are also unchanged for simplicity. The uncertainty factor is set as 
10, which means the variation range of pre-exponential factors is [ 0.1 ×
Ai, 10 ×Ai], where Ai is the pre-exponential factor of ith reaction. It is 
noted that GA with binary encoding method may fail when handling 
multi-dimensional, high-precision numerical problems due to the 
excessive search space, called curse of dimensionality. Therefore, Elliott 
et al. [40] proposed a GA with real code or floating point (FP) genes to 
deal with the chemical kinetics optimization problems. Since there are 
dozens of reactions needing modification, the real encoding approach 
was adopted in this study. Similar to mechanism reduction for initial 
conditions, the starting point (generation 1) of iteration is the reduced 
resulting mechanism. The important GA parameters were exhibited in 
Table 2.

4.Mechanism verification 
The detailed mechanism was processed by the GA, and a compact 
mechanism set (Mechanism 1) was extracted. After that, Mechanism 1 
was optimized to reduce disagreement between model predications and 
experiment measurements of IDTs and LFS obtained in literature. 
Although the global fitness of the reduced mechanism and the optimized 
mechanism was satisfactory as described above, the mechanisms still 
needed to be further verified against experimental data under a wide 
range of operation conditions.

4.2. Verification of laminar flame speed

4.4. Verification of the combustion in HCCI engine 
Different from the conventional CI combustion mode, the HCCI 
combustion process was mainly controlled by chemical reaction kinetics 
[52] as the homogeneous premixed mixture in-cylinder. The reduced 
mechanism was validated against the in-cylinder pressure data of the 
HCCI engine contributed by Pochet et al. [11]. The engine specifications 
and operating conditions were listed in Table 4. The calculation was 
done in commercial 3D CFD software and the RNG k-ε model was chosen 
for turbulence simulation. Due to the symmetry of the combustion 
chamber, a 60•grid was established to reduce computational cost. The 
2.0 mm base grid and mesh adaptive refinement are employed. The 
number of calculation grids was more than 400,000 when the engine 
was at the top dead center and was displayed in Fig. 19. The simulation 
began at the intake valve closing ( 127•CA BTDC) and ended at the 
exhaust valve opening (127 •CA ATDC). 
Fig. 20 compared the measured and predicted in-cylinder pressures 
at various hydrogen blend ratios. It can be observed that the peak in- 
cylinder pressure simulated by the initial mechanism, Mechanism 1 
and Mechanism 2 exhibited no obvious difference and the relative error 
between predicted and measured data was within 5%. The estimated 
ignition delay time by the three mechanisms was significantly different, 
and the simulated pressure curve by Mechanism 2 was closer to the 
experimental profile. This was due to the reduced mechanism being 
optimized to accurately forecast the ignition delay time under inter-
esting conditions. 5.Conclusion 
The reduced mechanism and optimized mechanism for ammonia/ 
hydrogen mixture were both developed through the single-objection 
genetic algorithm. The reduced mechanism included 29 reactants 
(including CO, CO2, CH4, C2H6 only defined as three-body colliders, 
inert gas Ar and He), and 63 element reactions. 
During the mechanism reduction progress, not only the IDT and 
steady-state temperatures were included as evaluation terms to keep 
high fidelity with the initial mechanism, but also the calculational cost 
time by the reduced mechanism was restricted to avoid stiff mechanism. 
The sensitivity analysis of ignition delay time illustrated the most 
important reactions to ignition which were retained in the reduced 
mechanism. This indicated that GA was a feasible methodology for 
reducing detailed mechanisms, which had flexible and adjustable eval-
uation rules and didn ’t require the user to understand the combustion 
chemistry. 
The reduced mechanism was optimized based on GA as the baseline 
of fundamental experimental data. The predicted IDTs and LFS, pre-A 
variation amplitude, and calculation time were considered compre -
hensively for optimization. The overall score showed that the optimized 
mechanism performed much better on forecasting LFS. In addition, 
mechanism verification and comparison were carried out in shock tube, 
laminar flame speed, JSR, premixed flame, and HCCI engine environ -
ment. The consistency between the predictions and the measurements 
demonstrated the reliability of the reduced mechanism and optimized 
mechanism.
